Neroli is often sold as brightness. On skin it is stranger than that: orange blossom, a little bitterness, and a clean line that either vanishes or becomes part of the collar.

We are interested in the third hour. The first is advertisement. The third is the person.

<h2 id="the-third-hour">The third hour</h2>

A cologne that is only sharp at the opening is a greeting, not a perfume. The one we kept had a soft powder under the citrus, enough to survive a wool coat and a warm room. It did not fill a corridor. It stayed where it was put.
