If you want a perfume that smells like a plain white bar of soap, pick two samples with different textures: a white musk oil and a soapy spray. Smell both once they have settled. The one you enjoy after an hour is a better buy than the one that smells cleanest in the first minute.

<h2 id="what-smells-soapy">What smells like a bar of soap?</h2>

In [a white bar soap discussion on r/Perfumes](https://www.reddit.com/r/Perfumes/comments/1xwe8wr/looking_for_an_affordable_white_soap_bar_perfume/), the shopper had already tried Bubble Bath and Cacharel Noa. Several commonly recommended clean scents came across as floral, lotion-like, or broadly fresh to them. Another reader found Noa even soapier than Glow by JLo. That disagreement makes the scent of your own soap a more useful reference than the word “clean” on a product page.

The replies also point to different kinds of soap. One reader described Tesori d’Oriente White Musk as close to a plain bar, with a slightly metallic edge. Another found the waxiness of Comme des Garçons Marseille distracting, while others liked how closely it evoked Marseille soap. Decide whether you enjoy that waxy edge, the scent of a creamy beauty bar, or just the soft musk left after rinsing.

<h2 id="how-to-sample">How to sample for a soap-like dry-down</h2>

1. Smell your reference bar and pick two words that describe it, such as creamy and powdery or waxy and crisp.
2. Try each perfume separately on a day you can skip scented lotion. Use the amount directed on the label and jot down what you smell after the opening and again about an hour later.
3. Before ordering a bottle, check whether the fragrance still smells like your reference bar or has shifted toward flowers, lotion, or laundry detergent. Compare small sizes and include delivery in the cost.
