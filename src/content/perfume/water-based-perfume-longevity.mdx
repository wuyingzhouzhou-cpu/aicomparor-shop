A water-based perfume can be noticeable on your skin after its trail has faded. To decide if it lasts long enough for you, check two things: how long you can smell it up close, and how far away you can smell it. The useful answer comes from the specific formula on your skin.

<h2 id="wear">Check longevity and projection separately</h2>

In [a Reddit discussion about water-based perfume](https://www.reddit.com/r/Perfumes/comments/1y2abd0/does_anyone_have_any_experience_with_water_based/), a shopper loved a fragrance in body care and wanted to know how it might wear as a perfume. One commenter had tried the water and oil versions of Russian Caravan Tea from CB I Hate Perfume. On that person's skin, the water version stayed close and was detectable for about half a day. The oil lasted longer for them. [The brand explains its water and oil formats](https://www.cbihateperfume.com/faq). That one comparison gives you a concrete test to try, while other fragrances may wear differently.

<h2 id="formula">Read the label for the exact version</h2>

The product name tells you which ingredient list to check. [Dior describes Sauvage Eau Forte](https://www.dior.com/en_us/beauty/products/sauvage-eau-forte-C099800171.html) as alcohol-free and lists water first. When shopping, look for **Eau Forte** on the box and in the listing title. Sauvage Eau de Toilette, Eau de Parfum, and Eau Forte are different versions. For a different brand, check the actual ingredients if alcohol matters to you. A water-based description by itself leaves room for different formulas.

<h2 id="trial">Test a sample before ordering a bottle</h2>

1. Try the exact version on skin and follow the brand's application directions. Compare on days when you're wearing similar clothing and can avoid competing fragrances.
2. Check it shortly after applying, around lunchtime, and when you would normally consider reapplying. Write down whether you can smell it up close and whether you still notice it during ordinary movement.
3. Decide whether that experience suits your routine before committing. If you want an occasional close scent, a subtle dry-down may suit you. If you need a more noticeable trail, compare it directly with a scent you already wear. A tester or travel size makes that decision cheaper when available.

If you're comparing a spray with something you can apply discreetly during the day, see [solid perfume vs. rollerball](/perfume/solid-perfume-vs-rollerball).
