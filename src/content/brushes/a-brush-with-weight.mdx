A brush is a tool you forget, or a tool you fight. The ones worth writing about have a handle that sits, a ferrule that does not wobble, and hair that returns to a line after it is washed.

We judge them dry, in the hand, before any product is involved. If the object is unpleasant before it works, it will not become pleasant later.

<h2 id="in-the-hand">In the hand</h2>

Weight is not luxury. Weight is information. Too light and the brush skates. Too heavy and the wrist starts editing your face for you. The brush we kept this winter sat between the two, with a ferrule that felt finished rather than glued on as an afterthought.

<h2 id="before-color">Before color</h2>

Wash a new brush before you trust it. Shed hairs on the first rinse are a fact, not a verdict. What matters is whether the shape returns, and whether the handle still feels like something you want to pick up on an ordinary morning.
