For a broad patch of under-eye discoloration, a small curved head can press concealer into place with just a few taps. For one small spot, try a finer tip that deposits product only where you want coverage. The most useful detail in a product photo is how much of the head touches your skin when you turn it on its side.

<h2 id="shape">One curved head, two contact areas</h2>

The Haus Labs Concealer Brush illustrates the difference. The manufacturer describes a dome with a pointed tip. Lay the curve against the skin to blend the edge of a patch; turn the brush to use the tip beside the nose. With a small flat brush, place product using the narrow edge and press with the flat face. If the mark you want to cover is smaller than the head, keep the first dot small and blend only its perimeter. [Haus Labs brush design](https://www.hauslabs.com/products/concealer-brush).

<h2 id="feel">Why two people can disagree about softness</h2>

In a Sephora discussion, one reader liked the Haus Labs brush and another found it pokey. A third liked it despite usually noticing scratchy bristles. That disagreement makes an online star average a poor guide to this particular preference. If a clean tester is available, compare how the side feels against the back of your hand with how the tip feels upright. Use the amount of pressure you would actually use to apply makeup. [Read the concealer brush discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/Sephora/comments/1xzyq0i/best_concealer_brush/).

<h2 id="pack">What to check if it goes in your purse</h2>

The dome and tip only help if they reach your destination intact. A cap needs clearance around the widest part of the head. For a separate sleeve, slide it on in the direction of the hairs. Dry the brush fully before packing. If carrying two tools is the bigger nuisance, compare [dual-ended travel brushes and capped singles](/brushes/travel-makeup-brushes).
