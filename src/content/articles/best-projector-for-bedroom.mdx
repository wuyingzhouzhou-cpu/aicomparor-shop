Lamp off. Phone face-down on the nightstand. White wall catching a soft rectangle of picture while light from the hallway still leaks under the door—roommate cooking, or just the hallway bulb nobody turns off. That is the bedroom-projector brief: wall movies from a pillow, not a living-room ultra-short-throw cinema install.

**Short answer:** in a **dark** bedroom, take licensed apps (Netflix that actually opens), quieter fan figures as the brand lists them, and placement helpers—auto focus, auto keystone, a stand that tilts—over the biggest marketing lumen number. <h2 id="what-bedroom-projector-means">What “bedroom projector” actually means</h2>

A bedroom wall projector is usually a compact 1080p unit aimed at a painted wall or a pull-down screen from a nightstand, dresser, or low shelf. You are watching from a pillow—sometimes on your side—so angle flexibility and fan noise matter as much as punchy brightness. This is not the living-room UST category (laser short-throw boxes that sit inches from a 100-inch ALR screen). Different room, different filter.

People search *bedroom projector*, *best projector for bedroom*, *bedroom wall projector*, *bedroom movie projector*, *portable*. Same job: quiet enough to sleep near, bright enough once the curtains close, smart enough that you are not hunting an HDMI stick at midnight.

<h2 id="buying-filter">The bedroom buying filter (checklist)</h2>

Work these in order. Skip the ones that do not match your room.

1. **Brightness — prefer ISO 21118 / ANSI numbers.** Brands publish **ISO** or **ANSI** lumen figures; those are the useful comparisons. Marketing “LED lumens” often inflate the story and are not the same scale. A dark bedroom with curtains can live with a few hundred ISO/ANSI lumens. Daylight from a west-facing window changes the game—then you need more light or better blackout, not a louder brochure claim.

2. **Noise.** Brand dB figures are listed claims, not our measurement. Side-sleepers and light sleepers feel fan hum more than living-room viewers. Prefer quieter published numbers when models are otherwise similar—do not invent a “whisper quiet” score from a product shot.

3. **Auto focus / auto keystone / obstacle avoidance.** Nightstands are crooked. Remotes get lost under a duvet. ISA-style auto alignment, autofocus, and auto keystone save the “why is the picture a trapezoid” fight at 11 p.m.

4. **OS and licensed Netflix.** Google TV or Samsung Tizen with **licensed Netflix** (and other apps on the device) beats casting-only workflows when you just want to press play. Casting from a phone works; it is one more step when you are half asleep.

5. **Battery portable vs plug-in.** Battery cans move from suitcase to nightstand. Plug-in units stay put and often pair with USB-C power banks for short sessions—check the brand’s Type-C / power-bank notes before you assume runtime. Do not invent battery minutes beyond brand claims.

6. **Throw ratio.** Many compact bedroom units sit near a **~1.2:1** standard throw (roughly: distance ≈ 1.2 × image width). Tiny rooms that need the unit inches from the wall need true short-throw or ultra-short-throw—most portable nightstand models are not that.

7. **Ceiling vs wall tilt for side sleepers.** A 130°–180° cradle or rotating lens lets you aim at the ceiling or high on the wall while your head stays on the pillow. Stands matter as much as lumens here.

<h2 id="placement-tips">Placement tips for wall movies</h2>

**Distance for ~80–100" at ~1.2:1.** At a typical ~1.2:1 throw, image width is roughly distance ÷ 1.2. For an ~80–100" diagonal (16:9 width ≈ 70–87"), expect on the order of **~7–9+ feet** of throw—measure your wall-to-nightstand gap before you buy. True short-throw is a different product class.

**Blackout helps more than brochure lumens.** Curtains that actually close beat arguing with a west window. A white or light-neutral wall is enough for many dark-room nights; a gray screen is optional, not required for sleepy viewing.

**Sound vs partners.** Built-in speakers are fine for solo nights. Shared walls or a sleeping partner usually means Bluetooth headphones or a small soundbar you can mute fast. Fan noise and dialogue volume are separate problems—solve both.

**Side-sleeper formula.** Aim high (ceiling or upper wall) with a tilting cradle or rotating lens → keep the unit on the nightstand, not under the bed → use headphones if someone else is asleep → leave a USB-C bank nearby only if the brand documents power-bank use.

<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2>

### Can a side sleeper project on the ceiling?

Yes—if the stand or lens rotates far enough (Freestyle-class 180° cradles, MoGo-class multi-angle stands, GV31-style rotating lenses). Confirm the tilt range on the PDP; not every “portable” aims straight up.

### ISO lumens vs ANSI lumens—what should I trust?

Prefer **ISO 21118** or **ANSI** figures the brand publishes. They are standardized brightness claims. “LED lumens” marketing numbers are a different, often inflated scale—do not treat them as interchangeable with ISO/ANSI when you compare models.

### Do I need a short-throw projector for a tiny bedroom?

Only if the projector must sit very close to the wall. Most bedroom portables are **standard throw (~1.2:1)**. Measure first. If you only have two feet of depth, look at true short-throw / UST living-room designs instead of forcing a can projector against the baseboard.

### Is casting enough, or do I need Google TV / Tizen?

Casting works. Licensed Netflix (and peers) on Google TV or Tizen is simply fewer steps at bedtime. If you already live in Apple TV / Fire Stick land, a dumb HDMI projector can still fit—just budget the stick and the remote clutter.
