At an equal 100-inch diagonal, a 16:9 TV and a 16:9 projection screen show the same size picture. Choose a TV if afternoon viewing with the curtains open is the everyday job. A projector becomes more attractive if you can darken the room, fit a substantially larger picture, or roll the screen away when the movie ends.

<h2 id="room">Mark the screen and leave room for speakers</h2>

A 100-inch 16:9 image is about 87 inches wide and 49 inches tall. Tape that rectangle on the wall, then mark your speaker positions and the bottom of the picture. A 120-inch image needs about 105 inches of width before adding the frame. In a home-theater discussion, one reader liked the idea of a much larger screen but described how front speakers limited the width available; another described putting speakers behind a suitable screen. If you want that arrangement, budget for an acoustically transparent screen and space behind it. [Read the 100-inch TV discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/hometheater/comments/1vouepo/would_you_still_buy_a_projector_now_that_100_tvs/).

<h2 id="daylight">Try the afternoon test as well as movie night</h2>

At the hour you usually watch, switch off the overhead lights and look at the screen wall. If it is still bright, dark areas of a projected image will share that background light. Decide whether closing curtains for an entire game or TV episode is realistic. In a dedicated evening room, check the lens-to-screen distance and whether people will cross the beam on their way to the door. [BenQ on sizing a projection setup](https://www.benq.com/en-us/knowledge-center/knowledge/build-a-home-theater-in-a-small-apartment.html).

<h2 id="budget">Compare two delivered, working systems</h2>

On the TV side, count delivery, the stand or mounting hardware and a route that clears turns and stairs with the boxed panel. On the projector side, count the screen, a stable shelf or mount, power, cable routing and any blackout treatment. Bring an existing sound system into both budgets consistently. If your current shelf is the deciding factor, use the [100-inch throw-distance calculation](/articles/100-inch-projector-throw-distance).
