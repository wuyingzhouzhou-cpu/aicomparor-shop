A Chelsea boot earns a page when it disappears into the day. Not the first hour, when the leather is still performing. The fourth hour, on a wet pavement, when you stop noticing the boot and start noticing the street.

<h2 id="after-the-rain">After the rain</h2>

We wore a single pair through a week of weather that could not decide. The elastic held its shape. The sole did not collect a theatrical amount of water. The shaft stayed low enough for a coat hem, which is a small mercy and also the whole point of this silhouette.

If you are learning how to look, start with the [guide to reading a boot](/guides/reading-a-boot). The claims on a product page are louder than the object.

<h2 id="what-we-kept">What we kept</h2>

We are not scoring comfort out of ten. We are asking a plainer question: would we reach for it again on a morning with no plans to be impressive?

The leather darkened honestly at the toe. The elastic did not bag. There is a slight weight, which we prefer to a boot that feels like a slipper and collapses by November.

> Keep the boot that still looks considered when you are only going as far as the corner.

<h2 id="a-note-on-commission">A note on commission</h2>

Some links in this magazine may earn a commission. That does not decide what stays. If a boot only looks right in the studio photograph, it does not get the paragraph.
