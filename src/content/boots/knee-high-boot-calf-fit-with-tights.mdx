Wear the tights you plan to use, find where the boot opening will land, and measure your leg **at that height**. Your widest calf measurement alone can mislead you. Two boots with the same opening can sit quite differently if one ends near the knee and the other stops on the fullest part of the calf.

<h2 id="measure">Measure the shaft and your leg at the same height</h2>

Use a pair you already own as a reference. Stand it upright and measure from the top of the heel along the shaft to the opening. Check how the retailer defines shaft height, and whether the measurement comes from your shoe size. Then measure around your leg with the tape level and resting lightly on the tights. If your legs differ, write down both measurements. A circumference is the distance around the opening; a flat measurement across the top is only half that distance on a flexible, flattened shaft.

<h2 id="shape">How much space looks right with a skirt?</h2>

One Reddit shopper choosing boots for mini skirts and tights heard two plausible answers: an ankle boot would show more leg, while some readers preferred the taller silhouette. Another shopper asking about a tall pair got comments about a loose opening. Those are styling preferences. A straight riding shaft can stand away from the leg intentionally; a close stretch shaft gives a different outline. Try your usual hem in the mirror before deciding that a visible gap means you need a smaller shoe. <h2 id="try-on">Sit, walk and check the zipper</h2>

Sit with your knees bent. If the back edge presses into the crease, try a shorter shaft before changing calf width. If the zipper needs force halfway up, compare a wider shaft in the same foot size. If the foot slides forward while the top feels right, another last may fit better. For a structured reference, compare the [Franco Sarto Meyer riding boot with a western shaft](/boots/western-boots-vs-riding-boots). A boot shaper can hold a tall pair upright in storage; it cannot correct the fit on your leg.
