Product pages describe a life you have not lived yet. This is a way to read them without being rushed. It pairs with our note on [the quiet Chelsea](/boots/the-quiet-chelsea).

<h2 id="the-sole">The sole</h2>

Look at the sole before the story. A city boot needs a sole that meets pavement, not a lug meant for a hillside photograph. Ask whether the welt, if there is one, is something a cobbler can actually work on. If the page will not say how the sole is attached, treat that silence as information.

<h2 id="the-upper">The upper</h2>

Leather should be named. Premium is not a material. Calf, nubuck, and a coated fabric are different objects with different lives in rain. Elastic on a Chelsea should look dense, not like a strip of waistband.

- Name of the leather, not a mood.
- Where it is made, stated plainly.
- What happens when it gets wet.

<h2 id="the-claim">The claim</h2>

Waterproof, for life, and handmade are large words. Keep the pair that explains itself in smaller ones. Care is part of reading: a cream that darkens the leather evenly is more useful than a promise.
